How these numbers are made

Alex Points — the headline

A Bradley-Terry model where each game counts as a fractional win taken from the point margin. A 3-point win is worth about .59 of a win, a 17-point win about .85, a 45-point win about .97.

So margin still matters, but a 63-0 game is not worth nine times a 7-0 game. The number is in points: the difference between two ratings is the expected margin on a neutral field.

BT (W/L) — record only

Textbook Bradley-Terry on wins and losses, margin discarded entirely. Immune to running up the score, and blind to how close anything was.

Early in the season this column is nearly flat, because a single win or loss is one bit of information and every team has exactly one.

Massey — raw margin

Least squares on point differential, with no diminishing returns at all. It uses every point, which is why it separates teams earliest — and why it rewards a running-clock blowout against a weak opponent.

Where Massey and the headline rating disagree sharply, a team's margins are doing more work than its schedule justifies.

Harbin — the official one

OHSAA's playoff qualifier, shown for comparison. It counts wins weighted by opponent division and by the wins of teams you beat. It ignores margin and gives nothing at all for a loss, however narrow.

It answers "who earned a playoff spot", not "who is best". The two questions have different answers.

Shrinkage

Every model is pulled toward the field average by a fixed prior worth about 1.5 pseudo-games. This is what makes the math solvable at all in Week 1, when the unregularized estimate is infinite.

The prior does not decay on a schedule — real games simply outgrow it. By Week 8 it is noise.

Same-name schools

Ohio fields three schools called Northwest, three called Perry, three called Crestview, and a long tail of North / South / East. Names are matched to schools using each team's running record and the regions its opponents come from.

Where that is not decisive the schools are kept separate and tagged ? — never merged, because merging two programs silently corrupts every rating that touches them.
